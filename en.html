Privacy Policy – Start Force One

Version 4.4 · Last updated: [date of publication] · App: Start Force One (com.startforce.one)

1. In short

  • Everything you record stays on your phone. Videos (no sound), athletes and results are stored on the device. We run no servers, never see your data, and the app contains no advertising, analytics or tracking. The usage-statistics feature built into two bundled Google libraries is switched off (section 8).
  • Nothing leaves the phone except: Android's backup of the app data to your Google account (if enabled), your photo app's cloud sync of the videos, files you export or share, the Pro check with Google Play at every start, the two-phone transfer you set up (a connected finish phone sends its videos to the start phone) and – if you use it – the link to a self-built light barrier.
  • Two-phone timing sends the finish video encrypted and directly to your second phone — never through the internet or a server.
  • Google Play is contacted only to check or process the optional Pro purchase.
  • You are responsible for consent from the people you film — for minors from a parent or guardian (older minors who understand what they agree to may consent themselves where your law allows; when in doubt, involve the parents).

4. Where the data is stored

Results, athletes, teams, tracks and settings are kept in the app's private database on the phone. Videos are saved to the phone's gallery (folder Movies/StartForce) so you can view and manage them like any other video. Pose detection (MediaPipe) runs entirely offline on the device. The app also keeps its diagnostic logs, finish videos not yet transferred and copies of your exports (for up to 24 hours) in its private storage. No data is stored on servers of the developer.

5. What can leave the phone

  • Android backup. If device backup is enabled in Android, the system backs up the app's database and settings (the whole database — athletes, teams and notes, tracks, all run data including the tracked body points — and the settings including the name of this phone and the pairing code — not the videos and not the purchase status) to the phone's Google account, restores them after a reinstall and copies them when setting up a new phone (Android Auto Backup / device transfer). This is an Android feature you control: Android settings → Google → Backup (wording varies by manufacturer). The backup copy remains in your Google account after the app is uninstalled until Google lets it expire or you delete it (Google One / Drive → Backups). The backup is end-to-end encrypted with your screen lock — provided a screen lock (PIN, pattern or password) is set — so Google cannot read it. Google provides the backup as part of your Google account under its own terms; the developer has no access. If you act for a club, check whether backing up athlete data to a private Google account — for which no data processing agreement exists — is compatible with your obligations, or switch the backup off.
  • Your photo app. Because the videos are in the gallery, a photo app with cloud sync (e.g. Google Photos) may upload them according to its own settings. Disable backup for the folder Movies/StartForce there if you do not want that.
  • Your exports. PDF reports, the app's backup archive (Settings → Backup & Restore) and the diagnostic logs (Help → Crash Log / Sync Log; the sync log may contain the local IP address of your second phone and technical identifiers of the connection) are handed to the app you pick in Android's share dialog. What happens to them from there is up to you. PDF reports contain the athletes' names, and the file name of a single-athlete PDF carries the athlete's name. Exported files are not encrypted by the app — store them accordingly. Publishing recordings or reports (e.g. on social media or the club website) additionally requires the athletes' or parents' consent under image rights (in Austria § 78 UrhG), independently of data-protection consent.
  • Two-phone timing. The finish video and the details needed to match it (run number, race distance, timing signals and the name of each phone — the phone model or the name you set under Settings → Two-Phone Finish → Name of this phone; no athlete names) go directly from one phone to the other, either over a Wi-Fi Direct connection or over a local Wi-Fi network both phones are on. No server and no third party is involved. During a session the start phone announces itself (service name, port and a random session token) on the Wi-Fi network or, over the direct link, to phones in radio range, so the finish phone can find it. Only phones on which the pairing code shown by the start phone has been entered can connect; the code itself is never transmitted. The whole transfer including the videos is encrypted: both phones negotiate a new key for every session (AES-256-GCM), on Wi-Fi as well as over Wi-Fi Direct. The start phone stores the finish phone's name with each run, so it ends up in the start phone's backup; the finish phone keeps its own copy of every finish video in its gallery — if that phone belongs to another person (e.g. an assistant coach), that person receives the finish videos.
  • Self-built light barrier (optional). If you enable the light barrier in Settings, the phone connects to the light barrier's own Wi-Fi network, which has no internet access, and exchanges only timestamps and signal levels over it. No videos, names or other personal data are sent to the light barrier. The feature is off by default. The connection is protected by the light barrier's Wi-Fi password (WPA2); additional signing of the messages is in preparation.

6. Google Play (optional Pro purchase)

Pro is a one-time purchase processed through Google Play (in the EEA: Google Commerce Ltd. / Google Ireland Ltd.; see the Google Play Terms of Service) and tied to the phone's Google account. At every app start the app asks Google Play whether Pro is unlocked and retrieves the current price; "Restore purchase" asks again, "Buy" opens Google Play's purchase dialog; the app needs internet access for this. Google sees the purchase and your Google account — never your recordings, athletes or results. Payment data are processed exclusively by Google under the Google Privacy Policy; the developer receives no payment details. The purchase status is stored locally on the phone. Your three free runs are counted on the phone only; this counter and the purchase status are excluded from the backup.

8. No processing by third parties, no tracking

The app uses no advertising IDs, no crash or usage analytics and no cloud AI. Two bundled Google libraries — MediaPipe for pose detection and the Google Play billing library — contain a feature that can send technical usage statistics to Google; this feature is switched off in the app. The only third party the app communicates with is Google Play for the Pro purchase (section 6). Android's backup (section 5) is a system service you control, not a transfer by the app.

9. Recording other people (club and youth context)

If you record athletes — especially minors — you as the user (coach, club) must inform them in advance, obtain their consent and handle the recordings according to the data-protection law that applies to you. Tell athletes and parents in writing who you are, what you record and why, how long you keep it, whether it is backed up to your Google account and that they can request access or deletion at any time (Art. 13 GDPR). For minors, obtain the consent of a parent or guardian; depending on the law that applies to you, older minors who understand what they agree to (in Austria usually from 14) may consent themselves — when in doubt, involve the parents. Consent only counts if it is genuinely voluntary: athletes or parents must be able to decline or withdraw without disadvantage for training, selection or team membership — offer an alternative such as training without recording. Body weight requires the explicit consent of whoever may consent (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR) — we recommend obtaining it in writing. Keep recordings of minors only as long as training needs them. The app shows a consent notice on every phone at first start.

10. Children

The app is aimed at coaches and athletes, not at children as independent users. Where data of minors are recorded, section 9 applies.

11. Retention and deletion

How long data are kept is your decision. To remove everything: (1) delete runs, athletes and teams in the app; (2) delete the videos in the gallery (Movies/StartForce); (3) delete exported PDF or backup files; (4) if Android backup was active, delete the app's backup copy under Google One / Drive → Backups; (5) if you used a second phone as finish camera, delete the finish videos there as well (gallery and finish camera → "Waiting to send"). Uninstalling the app removes its database and settings from the phone, but not the videos in the gallery, the exported files or the Google backup copy.

12. Your rights

Athletes exercise their rights — access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20), objection (Art. 21) and withdrawal of consent — towards the coach or club that made the recordings, because only there are the data held. Consent can be withdrawn at any time with effect for the future. Towards the developer these rights apply to the purchase and support data described in sections 2 and 6; for purchases we hold no name, only an order number — to exercise your rights regarding purchase data please quote your Google Play order number (GPA.…) from the Play receipt, otherwise we cannot identify you (Art. 11(2) GDPR). Everyone has the right to lodge a complaint with a data-protection supervisory authority — in their country of residence or in Austria with the Datenschutzbehörde (DSB), Barichgasse 40–42, 1030 Vienna (dsb.gv.at).
